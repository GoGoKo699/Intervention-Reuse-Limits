**24 September 2026.** The new argument separates two tasks: reproducing two prescribed initial/final laws and reproducing an entire controlled process. Only the former is required by the present experiment. Matching a finite set of observations, positive realization, matrix logarithms and perturbation expansions are established ideas. The claim here is the explicit shared three-state continuous-time construction and its quantitative comparison with the ordinarily reversible class on this specified physical rate family.

## Primary mathematical comparisons

**Robert B. Israel, Jeffrey S. Rosenthal and Jason Z. Wei, “Finding Generators for Markov Chains via Empirical Transition Matrices, with Applications to Credit Ratings,” Mathematical Finance 11 (2001), 245–265.** [Author page](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~israel/irw/index.html), [inspected author manuscript](https://www.probability.ca/jeff/ftpdir/wei.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9965.00114).

Sections 2–3 of the manuscript distinguish computing a logarithm from obtaining a valid Markov generator: zero row sums and the exponential identity do not guarantee nonnegative off-diagonal rates. Section 2 also describes the positive-real-spectrum logarithm calculation. Our realization proof addresses that same embedding requirement explicitly. Its three-eigenvalue interpolation reduces all generator signs to scalar logarithmic bounds and entrywise inequalities. It does not infer continuous-time validity merely from a positive transition matrix, and makes no novelty claim for the embedding problem or spectral logarithm.

**Qingqing Huang, Rong Ge, Sham Kakade and Munther Dahleh, “Minimal Realization Problems for Hidden Markov Models.”** [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.3698), [inspected full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.3698).

The abstract and Sections I–II distinguish finite-string inputs, realization of an entire stationary output process, and learning from finite observations. Their generic HMM realization results concern a different task. Our construction fits two controlled two-time tables, imposes a shared deterministic binary readout and stationary field tilt, and proves positive continuous-time generators locally. It neither reconstructs the entire output process nor establishes a learning algorithm for unknown parameters. The finite-sample test separately rejects the prescribed ordinarily reversible null; it does not estimate the three-state predictor.

## What is specific to this continuation
