## 1. Direct joint-law witness

Reviewers checked the chronology of both protocols, the balanced binary joint-table representation, and the factor of two between joint TV and maximum final-mean/correlation error. With biased initial marginals the equality is not used; the one-way bounded-moment implication remains valid.

The direct polynomial is the earlier conditional-covariance identity after replacing high-preparation expectations by low-preparation joint moments. Both singleton possibilities are included. The positive general three-state predictor shares the closed mean equations and the conditional initial coordinates in each visible sector, establishing the two joint-law upper without claiming arbitrary multitime path-law equality.

The nominal lower excludes a full four-moment box. The rounded ordinary comparator is checked as a positive, stationary, detailed-balanced generator pair with exactly the prescribed Gibbs tilt. Its two joint laws provide an achieved upper. Neither the local fits nor the necessary polynomial alone characterize all realizable ordinary response tables.

## 2. Stationary-law, execution and detector errors

Separate derivations checked the biased direct identity and the three high-field stationarity/adjoint defects. When different hypotheses may use different calibrated channels, both channel uncertainty budgets are charged. The constructive three-state predictor uses the reference channel and receives the conservative error allowance $4\times10^{-5}$. No uncounted persistent detector memory is admitted.

## 3. Fixed-score tests

The endpoint test was independently reviewed at proof and source level. Its occupation-coordinate tangent has an exact bilinear remainder. Localized nuisance bands, preparation/model-error transfer, population variance bounds and target displacement all use consistent mean/occupation factors. Independent pinned runs reproduce its 59-check canonical report byte for byte.

The snapshot proof retains within-trial correlation: each entire initial/final score is one summand. Its exact second moment depends on the initial mean, while the target variance subtracts the correct squared score mean. Reference detector inversion amplifies observed-TV model error by the product-channel inverse norm. The inverse table need not be positive; only its signed-TV norm and linear moments are used. The physical and noisy rows cover their stated target nuisance classes as well as the ordinary null.

Both tests fix their centers, coefficients, gates, allocations and thresholds before collecting data. For any fixed null law, either it lies outside the population box and gate passing is rare, or it lies inside and at least one valid score inequality controls rejection. These are alternative parameter cases. Their errors are not added. Requiring both sign tests to reject also needs no division of the type-I budget between signs. Target-power failures do require a union bound over scores and gates, and that allocation is explicit.

The exponential-moment derivations use bounded independent summands. No asymptotic normal approximation, fitted standard error, simulated power or claimed iid consequence of finite waiting is used. Deterministic calibration bounds remain premises; obtaining them statistically would require an additional confidence budget.

## 4. Resource and scientific limits

The endpoint counts are single-readout trials with two equilibrium preparations. Snapshot counts are paired trials with two readouts each and one preparation type. The designs therefore do not have a single interchangeable sample unit. The 62/36 reset waits apply to the known target, not uniformly to arbitrary rival rates. Measurement overhead and device-specific time scales remain unpriced.

The necessary paired-sample lower uses a fixed feasible ordinary comparator and the adaptive information chain rule, separately from the sufficient tangent-score designs. Its integer rounding applies only to a fixed budget; an expected stopping-time lower remains real-valued. Common known detector noise can only reduce the comparator information. No optimality claim follows.

The mechanism is short and the nominal deterministic gap is tightly bracketed. The statistical improvements do not establish that the hidden-state preparation and disturbance promises can be implemented. The primary-source comparison remains bounded and the earlier Falk full-text lead unresolved. Manuscript drafting is deferred; no PRL-readiness claim is made.
