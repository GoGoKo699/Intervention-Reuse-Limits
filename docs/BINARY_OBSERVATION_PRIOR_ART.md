# Binary observations and reversible realization: focused source comparison

**Research date: 23 September 2026.** This focused search compares binary hidden Markov realization, positive dwell-time decompositions, and the distinction between observed reversibility and hidden detailed balance. The repository now proves an [uncapped binary reversibility penalty](BINARY_UNCAPPED_REVERSIBILITY_LOWER_BOUND.md) on a new tagged target family. This source comparison does not independently establish that theorem or certify priority.

## Useful full-text comparisons

**Bruno, Yang and Pearson, PNAS 102 (2005), 6326–6331, “Using independent open-to-closed transitions to simplify aggregated Markov models of ion channel gating kinetics.”** [Primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1088360/), [publisher](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0409110102). Inspected Methods, “Manifest Interconductance Rank (MIR) Form,” “Canonical Forms and Detailed Balance,” and “Rank 1 Topologies.” Their unnumbered theorems state generic MIR existence/identifiability, preservation of detailed balance in the canonical forms, and preservation of nonnegative rates in the rank-one detailed-balance case. These simplify topology at fixed numbers of open and closed states. General canonical forms may require negative rates: the four-state C–O–O–C example cannot have an equivalent physical BKU form. Thus canonical coordinate changes are not automatically admissible Markov reductions. This is a direct precedent for the distinction between algebraic realization and positive reversible realization. Its stationary binary dwell-time data and parameter counts do not give the repository's controlled-mean finite-error state penalty. The main article was inspected; the separately numbered proofs in its supporting PDF were not retrieved (cookie redirect).

**Flomenbom and Silbey, PNAS 103 (2006), 10907–10910, “Utilizing the information content in two-state trajectories.”** [Primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1544147/), [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-bio/0703013). Inspected Methods, “Rank of φₓ,ᵧ(t₁,t₂) and its Topological Interpretation,” “RD Form,” and Concluding Remarks. The positive first-passage decomposition separates initial substates, final substates, and cross-color transition channels. Reduced-dimensions forms use the ranks of joint dwell-time kernels, but their edges carry generally nonexponential waiting-time distributions; the resulting representation is generally not Markovian. Its small substate count therefore does not furnish a small finite CTMC competitor when every phase is counted. The article idealizes noiseless, infinitely long, perfectly resolved trajectories. It does not provide a uniform inverse bound from controlled scalar means. Its correction changes the rank bound to $\min(M_x,N_y)$; use the corrected version when discussing that formula. [Correction record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1564259/). The main HTML was inspected; the correction was available in a primary search excerpt, not full text, and supporting material was not retrieved.

**Alexander Schönhuth, “Generic Identification of Binary-Valued Hidden Markov Processes,” arXiv:1101.3712v6 (2013).** [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.3712v6). Inspected Theorem 1.3, Proposition 3.3, §§5–6, and equations (6.11)–(6.12). Given exact binary string probabilities through length at least $2d-1$, Theorem 1.3 identifies a generic d-state HMM up to hidden-state permutation, allowing an exceptional algebraic set where it cannot decide. Proposition 3.3 supplies the standard finite-word rank factorization; finite Hankel rank yields exact linear realization. There is no uniform noisy inverse modulus, no CTMC embeddability, no prescribed field rule, and no reversible-versus-unrestricted state comparison. Deterministic emissions and specially symmetric targets require care with generic hypotheses. Useful for attributing binary word realization and rank, not for replacing the positive entropy argument.

**Yong Chen, “On the reversibility of the observed process of three-state hidden Markov model,” arXiv:1101.5482 (2011).** [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.5482). Inspected Theorems 3.3–3.4, Example 1, and Proposition 3.8. Theorem 3.4 characterizes time irreversibility of the observed process for a three-state continuous-time hidden model using irreversibility of the hidden chain and pairwise distinct emission rows. Example 1 shows that any deterministic binary observation of a three-state chain has a time-reversible observed law, because two hidden states share an emission row. This is a warning against identifying observed reversibility with hidden detailed balance. It does not assert the existence of a reversible hidden realization, nor a bound on its state count. The repository asks about ordinary hidden reversibility, not a test of observed time asymmetry.

**Ivo Siekmann, Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 88, 2 (2026), “Modelling ion channels with a view towards identifiability.”** [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11538-025-01558-3). Published online 9 December 2025. Inspected §§3.4.1–3.4.4 and §3.5. The detailed three-state calculation shows that an algebraically equivalent sparsified model may have a negative rate. Whether an acyclic physical representative exists is linked to mixture versus signed-mixture exponential dwell laws and detailed balance. Section 3.5 compares extra mechanistic constraints on concentration dependence. It reinforces the need to preserve the specified actuator rule across interventions, beyond equality at one operating point. This is a low-dimensional identifiability calculation, not a finite-error order-separation theorem. Some formulas are rendered as images; only claims supported by the readable section text are used here.

## Comparison with the completed binary theorem

The classical tools most relevant to the construction are positive path decompositions, products of emission projectors and propagators, stationary adjoints, and positive changes of measure. Their use must be attributed separately from any new constrained conclusion. The [earlier source comparison](BOUNDED_WORD_RECOVERY_PRIOR_ART.md) covers the bounded-perturbation, analytic-continuation and coefficient-extraction ingredients; the present sources add binary realization and dwell-time context.

The [new target construction](BINARY_TAGGED_UNCAPPED_CONSTRUCTION.md) uses rare private leaves of one binary color to encode the old port types. The core has the other color, and distant ballast restores exact balance. Fixed nonnegative label-killing filters distinguish the leaf types. The proof supplies a target spectral gap and cap independent of family size, positive approximate gate action, and an explicit transfer budget uniform over rival rates and stationary masses. The fixed rate-to-gap ratio is very large; the construction does not retain the earlier binary budget or the nineteen-level target's tight spectral band.

The [mixed-word lemma](MIXED_KILLED_WORD_OBSERVABILITY.md) retains prescribed killed evolution between extracted smoothed projectors. The [weighted repair lemma](WEIGHTED_WHOLE_WORD_REPAIR.md) permits a small normalization mass without a global rival operator-norm bound. The complete theorem combines these interfaces with the tagged construction to prove a polynomial sufficient state count for unrestricted prediction and the reversible lower bound

$$
D_{\rm rev}^{\rm tag,unc}(\delta)
\ge \exp\!\left(\exp\!\left(c_H\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}\right)\right).
$$

The norm concerns actual controlled means with the prescribed field rule, preparation, readout and exact balanced binary histogram. Rivals may introduce new states with arbitrarily fast internal rates; every retained state is counted. The proof uses five physical fields with arbitrary positive durations and arbitrarily rapid switching. It does not give the lower bound at a fixed positive control clock or match the reversible upper growth. A polynomial unrestricted lower on the tagged family alone is not asserted: Section 7 of the theorem explicitly adjoins the earlier binary family to obtain a common-family polynomial growth class.

The source comparisons identify three limits. Generic identification gives no dimension-independent noisy inverse modulus. Canonical similarity transformations need not preserve nonnegative rates or the actuator rule across protocols. A nonexponential dwell-time edge is not a free replacement for its counted Markov phases.

The [binary boundary note](BINARY_UNCAPPED_RESEARCH_BOUNDARY.md) also distinguishes literal palindromic replacements from general binary words. None of the inspected source theorems supplies the complete combination of this positive encoding, uniform controlled-mean estimate and entropy lower bound. This is a bounded-search comparison of assumptions and conclusions, not a certification of priority or external validation of the repository's proof.

Search scope: both available search systems were used, with queries around reversible binary HMMs, aggregated CTMC realization, dwell-time ranks, first-passage decompositions, and positive realization. Several stronger-search queries returned unrelated results and were discarded. This is a bounded comparison, not an exhaustive review.
