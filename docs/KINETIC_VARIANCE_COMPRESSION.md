# Kinetic variance controls the visible cost of hidden memory

[Finite target](FINITE_REVERSIBILITY_ADVANTAGE.md) · [Symmetry compression](SYMMETRY_AVERAGED_REVERSIBLE_COMPRESSION.md) · [General kinetic interface](GENERAL_KINETIC_INTERFACE.md) · [Earlier two-label Volterra closure](FINITE_FIELD.md) · [Source and scope audit](SIMPLE_PREDICTION_SOURCE_AUDIT.md)

**Analytic result, 23 September 2026.** Kinetic heterogeneity has two roles: it excites deviations from the stationary hidden distribution and reads those deviations back into the visible signal. The exact memory kernel contains one factor of the heterogeneous return rate at each end. This gives a uniform, finite-amplitude error bound proportional to its stationary variance. Faster hidden mixing reduces the error.

The resulting predictor has only two states and is ordinarily reversible. The theorem allows arbitrary finite hidden size and stationary nonreversible dynamics, including field-dependent hidden generators with a common stationary law. It concerns controlled binary means for every allowed protocol and horizon, not an approximation to the whole visible path law. A conditional-variance version at the end also recovers the earlier nine-state symmetry bound. No novelty claim is made here.

## 1. Interface and two-state predictor

Let $\mu$ be a positive probability law on a finite hidden state space. Under a control $h$, let $K_h$ be a hidden generator with stationary law $\mu$, and take

$$
q_{iA}(h)=kb_i(h),\qquad
q_{Ai}(h)=k\mu_i r(h)b_i(h),\qquad k>0.
\tag{1}
$$

Assume $0<b_-\le b_i(h)\le b_+<\infty$ and $0<r(h)\le r_+<\infty$ on the allowed controls. The finite generators and protocols are assumed to define their usual time-dependent Markov evolution; bounded piecewise-constant controls are sufficient. A common upper bound on hidden rates is not required by the estimates. Prepare

$$
p_A(0)=a_0,\qquad p_i(0)=(1-a_0)\mu_i,
\qquad 0\le a_0\le1,
\tag{2}
$$

and read $S=-1$ at $A$ and $S=+1$ on hidden states. The repository's usual preparation is $a_0=1/2$.

For $\bar b(h)=\sum_i\mu_i b_i(h)$, define a two-state predictor with states $A,B$ and rates

$$
\widehat q_{AB}(h)=kr(h)\bar b(h),\qquad
\widehat q_{BA}(h)=k\bar b(h),
\tag{3}
$$

the same initial visible probability and readout. It is ordinarily reversible at each field, with stationary law $(1,r)/(1+r)$. Its single hidden state has internal generator zero, so it meets any nonnegative hidden exit cap. It does not retain a prescribed target barrier histogram or a prescribed nonzero sensitivity variance.

Averaging arbitrary barrier curves is allowed in the broad kinetic interface class. An average of exponentials need not be a single exponential on an interval. If observations use only $0,H$, with $b_i(0)=1$ and $H\ne0$, the predictor can instead use the exponential $\bar b(H)^{h/H}$: it has exactly the same rates at the queried fields. No interval-wide exponential-rule claim follows from endpoint matching.

## 2. Exact excitation, memory, and readout equations

Write $\langle u,v\rangle_\mu=\sum_i\mu_i u_i v_i$, let $K_h^*$ be the stationary adjoint acting on hidden densities, and put

$$
a=p_A,\quad f_i=p_i/\mu_i,\quad
z=f-(1-a)1,\quad c_h=b_h-\bar b(h)1,\quad
y=(1+r)a-1.
\tag{4}
$$

Both $z$ and $c_h$ have zero $\mu$-mean. With $\Pi_0u=u-\langle1,u\rangle_\mu1$ and $B_h=\operatorname{diag}(b_h)$, the full master equation gives exactly

$$
\begin{aligned}
a'&=-k\bar b\,y+k\langle c_h,z\rangle_\mu,\\
z'&=K_h^*z-k\Pi_0(B_hz)+k c_h y,
\qquad z(0)=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

Indeed, $f'=K_h^*f+kb_h(ra-f)$, and subtracting its mean gives the second line. The first line is the visible probability balance. Thus $c_h$ appears in both the source of hidden deviations and their contribution to the visible derivative.

On the centered subspace, let $G(t,s)$ be the evolution generated by

$$
L_t=K_{h(t)}^*-k\Pi_0B_{h(t)}.
$$

Variation of constants eliminates the hidden density exactly:

$$
z(t)=k\int_0^tG(t,s)c_{h(s)}y(s)\,ds,
$$
$$
\boxed{
a'(t)=-k\bar b(h(t))y(t)
+\int_0^t\mathcal M(t,s)y(s)\,ds,\qquad
\mathcal M(t,s)=k^2\langle c_{h(t)},G(t,s)c_{h(s)}\rangle_\mu.
}
\tag{6}
$$

The kernel generally depends on the intervening protocol; it is not asserted to be a stationary convolution or to have a fixed sign. Equation (6) is a finite-amplitude identity, not a response expansion. If every $b_i(h)$ is the same at each field, $c_h=0$ and the two-state reduction is exact, regardless of the hidden dynamics.

## 3. Uniform variance and mixing bound

Suppose the additive symmetric part of the hidden generator has a uniform centered gap $\gamma\ge0$, meaning

$$
-\langle u,K_h^*u\rangle_\mu\ge\gamma\|u\|_\mu^2
\quad\text{whenever }\langle1,u\rangle_\mu=0.
\tag{7}
$$

This assumption always holds with $\gamma=0$ for a stationary Markov generator: stationarity gives $-\langle u,K_h^*u\rangle_\mu=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\mu_i(K_h)_{ij}(u_i-u_j)^2\ge0$. It requires no ordinary reversibility; for a reversible generator it is the usual gap inequality. Define

$$
V=\sup_h\|c_h\|_\mu^2=\sup_h\operatorname{Var}_\mu b_h,\qquad
\lambda=\gamma+kb_-,\qquad
\chi=\inf_h(1+r(h))\bar b(h)>0.
\tag{8}
$$

Let $C$ be a uniform bound on $|y(t)|$ for the allowed preparations and protocols. The universally valid choice is $C=\max\{1,r_+\}$ because $0\le a\le1$.

**Two-state variance theorem.** The predictor (3) satisfies

$$
\boxed{
\sup_{T\ge0}\sup_{h(\cdot)}
|m(T)-\widehat m(T)|
\le\min\left\{2,\frac{2k C V}{(\gamma+kb_-)\chi}\right\}.
}
\tag{9}
$$

In dimensionless form, writing $g=\gamma/k$, the nontrivial bound is $2CV/[(g+b_-)\chi]$. It is independent of microscopic state count, minimum stationary mass, and observation horizon.

**Proof.** For centered $u$,

$$
\langle u,L_tu\rangle_\mu
=\langle u,K_h^*u\rangle_\mu-k\langle u,B_hu\rangle_\mu
\le-\lambda\|u\|_\mu^2.
\tag{10}
$$

Hence $\|G(t,s)\|_{2\to2}\le e^{-\lambda(t-s)}$. More locally, if (7) holds at a field-dependent rate $\gamma_h$ and $b_{\min,h}=\min_i b_i(h)$, then

$$
|\mathcal M(t,s)|\le k^2\|c_{h(t)}\|_\mu\|c_{h(s)}\|_\mu
\exp\!\left[-\int_s^t(\gamma_{h(v)}+kb_{\min,h(v)})\,dv\right].
\tag{11}
$$

Equation (5), or its upper norm derivative, now gives

$$
\|z(t)\|_\mu\le\frac{kC\sqrt V}{\lambda}.
\tag{12}
$$

If $e=a-\widehat a$, subtracting the two-state equation gives

$$
e'=-k(1+r)\bar b\,e+k\langle c_h,z\rangle_\mu,
\qquad e(0)=0.
\tag{13}
$$

Its damping is at least $k\chi$ and its forcing at most $k\sqrt V\|z\|_\mu$. Thus $|e|\le kCV/(\lambda\chi)$. Since $m=1-2a$, multiplying by two proves (9). This proves the claim directly for arbitrary switching; the usual finite-dimensional approximation extends it to allowed measurable protocols.

**Inactive-field refinement.** A field is inactive when $c_h=0$. At such a field both forcing terms in (5) and (13) vanish, so neither $\|z\|_\mu$ nor $|e|$ can increase. Consequently one may use the infima of $\gamma_h+kb_{\min,h}$ and $(1+r_h)\bar b_h$ only over active fields, and a bound on $|y|$ only when an active field is applied. The same constant-envelope proof gives (9) with those quantities. If all fields are inactive, the reduction is exact.

## 4. Two states suffice within about one percent for the finite target

For the unchanged twelve-state target of [the finite advantage theorem](FINITE_REVERSIBILITY_ADVANTAGE.md), use fields $0,H$ with $H=\log2$. At zero all barriers equal one, so only $H$ is active. The target has hidden gap at least $k$, and direct stationary averaging gives

$$
\bar b(H)=\frac{59}{128},\qquad
V=\operatorname{Var}_\mu b(H)=\frac{557}{49152},\qquad
\min_i b_i(H)=\frac38,\qquad r(H)=4.
\tag{14}
$$

For every two-field protocol, $a(t)\le1/2$. At the boundary $a=1/2$, the zero-field derivative is zero. At $H$, using $\max_i b_i(H)=11/16$ and $\sum_i p_i=1-a$,

$$
a'\le k\frac{11}{16}(1-a)-4k\frac{59}{128}a
=-\frac{37k}{64}<0\qquad(a=1/2).
$$

Thus the half-interval is invariant from the prescribed preparation. On the active field, $y=5a-1\in[-1,3/2]$, so $C=3/2$ suffices. The active-field constants are

$$
\lambda_H=\frac{11k}{8},\qquad
\chi_H=\frac{295}{128}.
$$

Substitution into (9) proves

$$
\boxed{
\sup_{T\ge0}\sup_{h(\cdot)\in\{0,H\}}
|m_T(h)-m_{\rm two\,state}(h)|
\le\frac{557}{51920}<0.01073.
}
\tag{15}
$$

The predictor has exactly one hidden state, zero hidden internal rate, and endpoint barriers $1,59/128$. The exponential $(59/128)^{h/H}$ realizes these endpoints within the original sensitivity range of this finite target. Thus (15) uses the same two-field controls, preparation, binary readout, original endpoint rate rule, and hidden exit cap $2k$. It does not preserve the target histogram; that is permitted in the finite comparison class. It supplies one predictor for all protocols and horizons, including every word on the original clock.

This upper does not assert that the optimal two-state error equals (15). It also does not contradict the finite ordinary-reversible lower: that theorem requires much finer accuracy. The result states a concrete observational limit on exposing this target's hidden state cost at coarse precision.

## 5. Conditional variance unifies block and symmetry compression

The same mechanism applies to a partition into $q$ hidden blocks. Let $\Pi$ be conditional averaging under $\mu$ onto functions constant on each block. Assume

$$
K_h\Pi=\Pi K_h\quad\text{for every allowed field}.
\tag{16}
$$

For a reversible hidden generator, strong lumpability of the partition is sufficient. Condition (16) also implies commutation with $K_h^*$. Arbitrary binning of barrier values does not ensure this condition.

Replace $b$ by its block average $\Pi b$ and take the exact quotient of the resulting model. It has $q+1$ total states, preserves the stationary preparation/readout convention and any hidden exit cap, and is ordinarily reversible when $K_h$ is. A field-independent $K$ gives a field-independent quotient; a general field-dependent $K_h$ need not. The one-block quotient of Sections 1–4 has zero hidden generator even in the latter case.

Set $\rho=\Pi f$, $z=(I-\Pi)f$, $c=b-\Pi b$, and $V=\sup_h\|c_h\|_\mu^2$. Starting with block-constant density, the residual equation is exactly

$$
z'=K_h^*z-k(I-\Pi)(B_hz)
+k c_i\,[ra-\rho_{\text{block}(i)}].
\tag{17}
$$

If $|ra-\rho_{\text{block}}|\le C$ and the residual subspace has symmetric gap $\gamma_\perp$, the energy proof gives $\|z\|_\mu\le kC\sqrt V/\lambda_\perp$, where $\lambda_\perp=\gamma_\perp+kb_-$. The bound $C=\max\{1,r_+\}$ is valid for preparation (2): the full density equation keeps $0\le f_i\le\max\{1,r_+\}$.

The discrepancy forcing of the coarse probability law is $(\sum_j d_j,-d_1,\ldots,-d_q)$, where

$$
d_j=k\sum_{i\text{ in block }j}\mu_i c_i z_i.
$$

Its total variation is at most $\sum_j|d_j|\le k\sqrt V\|z\|_\mu$. If the averaged coarse propagation contracts zero-mass rows in total variation at rate $\alpha>0$, variation of constants proves

$$
\boxed{\sup_{T,h}|m(T)-m_{\rm quotient}(T)|
\le\frac{2k^2CV}{\alpha\lambda_\perp}.}
\tag{18}
$$

A common return reset always permits $\alpha=kb_-$. A stronger contraction estimate can be substituted. The proof uses no minimum block mass.

For the previously proved [nine-state symmetry quotient](SYMMETRY_AVERAGED_REVERSIBLE_COMPRESSION.md), the only within-block barrier variance comes from the paired probes $p_3,p_4$:

$$
V=\frac16\left(\frac1{32}\right)^2=\frac1{6144}.
$$

Its verified occupation bound gives $C=2$, its residual damping is at least $\lambda_\perp=11k/8$, and its full-law contraction is $\alpha=9k/8$. Formula (18) immediately gives $1/2376$, exactly the earlier bound. The two-state and nine-state estimates are therefore two applications of the same variance mechanism.
